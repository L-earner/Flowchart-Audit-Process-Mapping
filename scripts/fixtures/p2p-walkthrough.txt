Purchase-to-Pay walkthrough — notes from meeting with AP and Procurement.

The process kicks off when a department requester raises a purchase requisition in
the ERP. The requester fills in the vendor, line items, quantities and cost centre.

The requisition then routes to the cost-centre manager for approval. The manager
reviews the requisition against the department budget. This approval is a key control
— it's a preventive, manual control performed on every requisition to prevent
unauthorised or over-budget spend. If the manager rejects it, the requisition goes
back to the requester to amend or cancel. If approved, it moves on.

Once approved, the procurement team converts the requisition into a purchase order
and issues it to the vendor. The system automatically checks that the PO value is
within the approved requisition amount before it can be dispatched — that's an
automated preventive control against PO/requisition mismatch.

The vendor delivers the goods and the receiving team records a goods receipt in the
ERP. When the vendor invoice arrives, an AP clerk performs a three-way match between
the purchase order, the goods receipt and the invoice. The three-way match is a
detective control, run per invoice, to catch quantity or price discrepancies and
prevent paying for goods not received.

If the three-way match fails, the invoice is parked and sent to procurement to
investigate the discrepancy. If it matches, the AP clerk posts the invoice for
payment. Finally, the payments team runs the weekly payment run to settle the
invoice, and the process ends.
